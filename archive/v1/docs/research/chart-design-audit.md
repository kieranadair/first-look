# Chart design rules and model guidance

This audit implements conditional chart guidance from Stephen Few's *Now You See It* (2020), Section II, against every chart family supported by First Look. Python enforces valid quantities, geometry and source mappings. Jev prioritizes supplied eligible choices; Terra proposes interpretations and executable checks. Neither model can grant an operation permission or override a rendering constraint.

The uploaded book was reviewed chapter by chapter against the current application, supplemented by the repository's article index and primary methodological/technical sources. Printed page references refer to the uploaded OCR's printed-page markers. The book is expert guidance; an application convention or engineering budget is not a universal experimentally validated threshold.

## Deterministic implementation and conditional judgment

| Chart family | Python owns | Guidance for Jev and Terra |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Magnitude bars and categorical facets | Zero is included for positive, negative and mixed quantities. An explicit comparison-domain override cannot silently truncate it. Category identity/order and recomputed Other retain their original meaning. Facets share numerical limits and use at most two panels per row. | Prefer precise position/length comparisons for ranking or closely spaced amounts. Decide whether a complementary definition or subgroup view addresses the question; do not invent exposure or an aggregation. Few Ch. 5, pp. 109–113; Ch. 10, pp. 235–238. |
| Categorical dots and correlation summaries | Generic dots use a padded local quantity domain. Correlation summaries explicitly use −1 to +1 and a correlation axis label. | A dot view can expose small differences far from zero; the appropriateness of that focus depends on the question. Preserve comparative category order. Few pp. 111–112; Robbins [2]. |
| Histograms | Preserve numeric interval boundaries, contiguous equal-width bins, zero-frequency bins, exact memberships and the declared count/proportion/density quantity. Native bar widths fill the actual bins. Stored median and IQR are annotated without estimating them from bar heights. | Histograms favour interval-frequency comparison. Bin width/origin are exploratory choices; no default is optimal for every distribution. Few pp. 144–147; Few [1]. |
| Frequency polygons | Use recorded bin midpoints, straight unsmoothed segments and the same quantity as the source histogram. Two-group comparisons compute common full-source bin edges and within-group proportions; both retain their own sample size and all bin memberships. No invented zero tails or KDE is added. | Lines can simplify shape comparisons for a few groups. Normalised shapes and raw count/exposure questions differ. The book's approximate curve-count guidance is not an eligibility law. Few pp. 146–150, 161–162; Few [1]. |
| ECDFs | Retain source-based cumulative proportions, right-continuous steps, certified display approximation and virtual endpoint extensions without row memberships. Individual threshold queries use source values. | Use cumulative/threshold perspectives alongside shape views where useful. An ECDF is not a ranked-value quantile plot or proof of a population distribution. Few pp. 141–148, 154–159; quantile conventions [5]. |
| Quantile deviations | Plot saved full-source estimates as quantile probability versus selected-minus-reference difference. Use a horizontal zero line and visible markers at the five saved probabilities; no interpolation curve, paired-row membership or uncertainty interval is invented. The exact table remains available. | Explain location, spread or tail differences as hypotheses in the supported range. A traditional Q–Q plot compares quantile values on both axes with an identity reference; rotating that reference would change the meaning. Few pp. 157–161; NIST [4]. |
| Grouped range boxes | Preserve recorded minimum, quartiles, median and maximum. Clearly label the min/max whisker convention and support. Do not silently substitute Tukey fences or call legitimate extreme observations errors. | Compare centre and spread while recognising that a five-number summary can conceal shape or mixtures. Few pp. 150–154; NIST [6]. |
| Scatterplots, influence and predictive diagnostics | The inner coordinate area is square, including on narrow containers. Unrelated axes retain their own padded numerical domains. Observed-versus-predicted views use shared compatible domains and identity; residual views show zero. Point/display-sampling memberships remain explicit. | Examine shape, spread, overdraw and unusual combinations before selecting a permissible check. A fit, high prediction score or low correlation does not establish a cause, independence or absence of structure. Few pp. 210–222. |
| Numeric density grids | Use a square coordinate area and stable numeric bin indices, including shuffled cell order. Preserve complete cells, interval bounds and source members; use a count colour legend. | A density companion can reveal overdraw in a scatterplot. Visible dense regions are not automatically latent clusters. Few pp. 218–222. |
| Categorical and model-confusion heatmaps | Keep category order consistent between native and model-review renderers. Counts use a sequential scale. Signed independence residuals use symmetric limits and a diverging colour scale with neutral zero. Display a numeric colour legend. | Distinguish counts, conditional proportions, exposure and residuals. Dark cells do not by themselves establish category dependence or a causal explanation. Few pp. 172–173, 235–238. |
| Chronological, conditional and indexed lines | Numeric elapsed time, existing gap/null semantics, shared comparison domains and recorded quartiles remain intact. Missing observations are not joined or converted to zero. Declared units and coverage cautions are visible. Indexed series retain their common reference and original values. | Choose raw chronology, a calendar companion or an eligible named smoother for the question. Do not choose an aspect ratio, smoother or transformation merely to strengthen an apparent trend. Unknown coverage does not prove that a final period is partial. Few pp. 177–201; Few [7]. |
| Treemap overviews | Area binds only original amounts from the existing validated concentration recipe: complete, nonnegative additive parts, identity transform and positive whole. Shares/total reconcile, pooled Other is refused, zero-area parts stay in the table, and overlapping full-credit entities are already blocked by the semantic engine. Stable part identities and native tree-index mapping preserve selections. No hierarchy or secondary colour measure is invented. | Prefer bars/dots for precise comparisons; use a treemap when a whole-family overview cannot fit as readable labelled rows. Few provides no universal category-count cutoff. The app exposes its panel-height/24px row budget and keeps the display choice editable. Few pp. 117–119; Shneiderman [3]. |

Square scatter areas are an intentional neutral convention supported by Few (p. 216) and his defaults essay [8]. This does not mean equal data units on arbitrary axes, and it is not a claim that square geometry is always empirically superior. Rensink's experiments/review [9] found similar correlation-perception performance under the tested compression conditions.

## Request policy and authority

`firstlook/visualization/guidance.py` separately records ten Python-owned application invariants and seven conditional preferences, with sources and a version. `policy_packet()` supplies a compact detached JSON policy; `guidance_for()` generates distinct role instructions.

- **Jev:** one atomic choice from the supplied eligible IDs, or a supplied abstention. It may prioritise a complementary view or clarification. It cannot add a chart, calculation, business meaning, executable option or numerical fact. Field values and descriptions remain untrusted data.
- **Terra:** inspect the supplied visual and tabular evidence, identify possible shape/scale/aggregation conflicts, propose only supplied Python checks, and label synthesis as interpretation. It cannot turn a visual impression into a measured result or a semantic permission.
- **Python:** validates the selected operation again, computes results from source rows, applies hard chart rules and preserves the exact artifact/context boundary. Prompt preferences never bypass these checks.

Both real provider adapters now include this policy in outgoing requests. Jev's recorded state/hash/instructions match its transmitted request; Terra records its policy-bearing packet and system instructions. Controlled SDK tests verify those request boundaries without paid model calls. The rule registry is also visible in an inspected chart's safeguards, separate from raw execution details.

No prompts ask models to perform arithmetic, decide whether a signed/nonadditive value can become an area, infer gap filling or rewrite a statistical estimator. Chart choices remain bounded by the app's finite operations; unavailable chart types are guidance examples, not newly authorised model outputs. A task-dependent preference becoming a deterministic default is explicitly labelled as an application design choice.

## Representation and reproducibility

The native compiler and offscreen analytical renderer share quantity binding, numeric domains, category order, grouped colours and colour limits. Square offscreen axes use a square box rather than equating unrelated units. Histogram annotations, reference lines and the three new templates are supported in both renderers. The review asset manifest records geometry, quantity binding and references; it makes no claim of pixel-identical font rasterisation.

`ChartSpec.value_field` distinguishes saved cumulative shares from original contribution amounts. Switching to ranked parts or a treemap preserves the original result artifact; pins retain the selected chart contract. Quantile deviations derive only from saved source-quantile tables. No model is required for any chart, and no local function executes generated code.

Maps, automatic geocoding, parallel coordinates, arbitrary KDEs, forecasts, automatic outlier removal and exhaustive chart generation remain outside the supported catalog. Spatial meaning still requires geographic identity, coordinate/region semantics and appropriate exposure; a suggestive field name cannot authorise a map.

## Supplemental sources from the index and primary references

1. **Stephen Few (2014), [Distribution Displays, Conventional and Potential](https://www.perceptualedge.com/articles/visual_business_intelligence/distribution_displays.pdf).** Complementary distribution representations and centre/spread annotations; expert guidance, not a universal ranking experiment.
2. **Naomi B. Robbins (2006), [Dot Plots: A Useful Alternative to Bar Charts](https://www.perceptualedge.com/articles/b-eye/dot_plots.pdf).** A guest article in Few's index. Accurate position comparisons, nonzero domains and consistent comparative ordering.
3. **Ben Shneiderman (2006), [Discovering Business Intelligence Using Treemap Visualizations](https://www.perceptualedge.com/articles/b-eye/treemaps.pdf).** Also a guest article. Overview/hierarchy affordances; example item counts do not define a universal threshold.
4. **NIST, [Quantile–Quantile Plot](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/qqplot.htm).** Matched quantile values, unequal sample sizes and identity-reference semantics.
5. **Hyndman and Fan (1996), [Sample Quantiles in Statistical Packages](https://robjhyndman.com/publications/quantiles/).** Estimator differences; First Look retains its recorded linear interpolation rather than silently adopting a new convention.
6. **NIST, [Box Plot](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/boxplot.htm).** Distinguishes min/max range boxes from fence variants.
7. **Stephen Few (2015), [Displaying Missing Values and Incomplete Periods in Time Series](https://www.perceptualedge.com/articles/visual_business_intelligence/missing_values_and_incomplete_periods_in_time_series.pdf).** Missing is not zero or a measured connecting segment. Coverage is a semantic declaration in this app.
8. **Stephen Few (2007), [The Role of Software and the Importance of Thoughtful Defaults](https://www.perceptualedge.com/articles/visual_business_intelligence/thoughtful_defaults.pdf).** Default square scatter coordinate areas and restrained non-data styling.
9. **Ronald A. Rensink (2017), [The Nature of Correlation Perception in Scatterplots](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-016-1174-7).** Evidence under particular dot distributions, sample sizes and compression conditions, not a universal application-layout result.
10. **[Apache ECharts chart sizing](https://echarts.apache.org/handbook/en/concepts/chart-size/)** and the installed Streamlit 1.67 ECharts reference. Native dimensions and media options are used without an authored CSS/JavaScript frontend. ECharts source was inspected for percentage bar-width semantics, label-bound shrinking and the treemap's virtual root.

The original uploaded book and third-party PDFs are not copied into the repository. This audit supports design and implementation choices; it does not establish a measured productivity gain or paid-provider uplift.
